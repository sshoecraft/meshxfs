## Verdict: REJECT

The control and the fix-on batch are sound. The control reverted only `tests/suite/fsx.sh`'s log handling on the frozen tree and reproduced 10 of 10, every lap exposed. The fix-on batch was sized correctly from that rate (N = 6, recomputed) and passed 6 of 6, every lap exposed, on the frozen candidate.

The cause this removal would publish is not proven as stated:

1. **"its EXIT trap removed /tmp/fsx.$$.*".** This rests on reading the old code. The control reverted the `/tmp` redirect and the trap together, and its lap cannot tell "written to `/tmp`, then removed" from "never written". No cited evidence shows the old script's `/tmp/fsx.<pid>.<ops>.log` existing with fsx's output, or the trap removing it.
2. **"so a failing check's log was gone before run.sh pulled the row's evidence".** This is unmeasured: the lap reads the node, not the pull (`tests/fsx_tmp_log_lap.sh:7-8`). It also does not fit run.sh, whose pull reads only `/root` (`run.sh:2273`), so a `/tmp` log would never have been pulled anyway.

**What is missing.** One of these:

- **Either** measure the mechanism. For example, run one lap with the reverted fsx.sh whose EXIT trap alone is left at the fixed form (output still to `/tmp/fsx.$$.<ops>.log`, the trap no longer removing it), and show it PASS with `/tmp/fsx.<pid>.<ops>.log` kept holding fsx's usage text. Or trace the reverted row on the node (e.g. `strace -f -e trace=openat,unlinkat` around `bash tests/suite/fsx.sh`) and show the `/tmp` log created and then unlinked by the trap's `rm`. Then keep the trap clause in the cause.
- **Or** resubmit with the cause reduced to what the control measured. In fsx.sh's old log handling, output went to `/tmp/fsx.$$.<ops>.log` and the EXIT trap pattern-matched `/tmp/fsx.$$.*`. With that handling, no failing check's fsx output was on the node after the row (10 of 10 exposed laps, b20261023-955af2). With the fix's handling, both were kept in `/root` (6 of 6, b20261023-349597). Drop the claims that the trap was the deleter and that the deletion is why run.sh's pull got nothing.

No new control or verification batch is needed for the second route. Both batches above already meet their conditions.
