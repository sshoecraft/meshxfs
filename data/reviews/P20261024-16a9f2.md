## Check 4: does the fix in the tree reach the whole cause? NOT MET

The record's defect is the lap grading on a kernel-log read from the ring after the lap's mark has rotated out: "the lap grades 'stage=no-fence' VACUOUS (an unexposed PASS) on a read that held nothing". The proposal says of the fix that "a read that does not hold both marks FAILs stage=kmsg instead of grading the fence", and that "the fix is configuration-independent". The script in the tree does not do that.

1. A second ring read, anchored on the same mark, still decides a verdict:
   - `tests/noino_fence_efd_hold_lap.sh:134` `"echo $HOLD_MS > $PARM/dbg_efd_hold_ms; nohup setsid rm -f $D/F ... for t in \$(seq 1 30); do n=\$(dmesg | sed -n '/$MARK/,\$p' | grep -ac 'P-EFD-HOLD start='); [ \$n -gt 0 ] && break; sleep 0.5; done; echo HOLD_SEEN=\$n"`

   If the mark has rotated out of S's ring by the time of this read, `sed` prints nothing and the result is `HOLD_SEEN=0`. That happens whatever the followed log holds.

2. That verdict comes before the whole-read check:
   - `tests/noino_fence_efd_hold_lap.sh:170` `if [ "$HOLD_SEEN" != 1 ]; then`
   - `tests/noino_fence_efd_hold_lap.sh:172` `echo "RESULT: VACUOUS label=$LABEL stage=hold wall=$(el)s evidence=$OUT"; exit 3`
   - `tests/noino_fence_efd_hold_lap.sh:174` `if [ -n "$unread" ]; then` (the stage=kmsg FAIL at `:175-176` comes after it)

   So a lap whose hold read missed the mark grades VACUOUS (`exit 3`, an unexposed pass) instead of failing. That includes a lap whose followed log is empty or not whole, for example because the follow never started: its start at `:113` sends output to `> /dev/null` and nobody checks it. This is the record's failure, a VACUOUS verdict on a kernel-log read that held nothing, reached through the lap's other ring read.

3. Neither batch exposed this site. In the fix-on followed logs, S's `P-EFD-HOLD start=` line comes 2.77-5.04 MB (14552-25779 lines) after the mark. For example, `tests/evidence/20261024T132952Z_noinoefd_nkv4/test1_kmsg.txt` has the hold at line 14552, 2765687 bytes in. The ring survived about 16.13 MB (89108 lines), so the hold read runs at 17-31% of the ring's capacity at 2/net/mesh/direct. Every lap of both batches shows `STAGE hold seen=1`, which means the batch's probe (`^RING-ROTATED: `, `tests/noino_fence_efd_hold_lap.sh:164-165`, measured after the run) never exercised the hold read. The record's reach is `1/any`, and the proposal's own argument says the defect appears where the log volume rotates the ring. A configuration with about 3x this volume in the run's first 15-25 s rotates the mark out before line 134, and the lap then grades VACUOUS again.

Removal under fixed-and-verified needs a patch that targets the proven cause, verified by testing that exercises the cause. The cause, the ring rotating past the mark before a ring read, is proven. The patch covers one of the lap's two ring reads, and the verification exercised only that one.

## Verdict: REJECT

Checks 1-3 are met for the read taken after the run. The record still cannot be removed as fixed, because the fixed lap can still grade VACUOUS on a kernel-log read from the ring after the mark rotated out. That path goes through the hold detection at `tests/noino_fence_efd_hold_lap.sh:134`, whose verdict at `:170-173` comes before the whole-read FAIL at `:174-177`. The proposal's statement that a read lacking both marks FAILs stage=kmsg is false for that path, and the fix-on batch never exposed it (hold reads at 17-31% of ring capacity, `hold seen=1` in all 16 laps).

What is missing before a remove --fixed can be approved:
- the hold detection decided from the followed log, or the whole-read check (and a check that the follow started) placed before any VACUOUS verdict, in the tree;
- a control and a verification sized from it that show the change: for example, a hold read made late enough, or a ring small enough, for the mark to rotate out before it, with the fix off failing and the fix on passing;
- or direct evidence that no ring read anchored on the mark is left in the lap's verdict path.
