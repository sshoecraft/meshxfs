## Check 1: the cause is proven by instrumented evidence, not inference. FAILS

The proposal says the cause is that the REGISTER "could sit at the target ahead of the node's writes on that nexus". The lap's own oracle separates that hazard from a plain command count:

- `tests/late_register_hold_lap.sh:38-40`: `FAIL     writes_done > 0 (writes the node sent down the late path while` / `its REGISTER was unresolved: the hazard), or requests_sent > 0` / `(any command, a path checker's TEST UNIT READY included)`

The writes count is zero in every control lap, on both configurations:

- `tests/evidence/lanes/g2b_a22_20261024T204638Z/step6.log:5` (laps 2-10 at :6-14 are the same): `lap 1/10: FAIL ... writes_done=0 requests_sent=7 held=0`
- `tests/evidence/lanes/g2b_a22nm_20261024T213527Z/step5.log:5-14`: all ten `writes_done=0 requests_sent=8` (lap 5: `requests_sent=7`) `held=0`
- Control precheck `tests/evidence/lanes/g2b_a22_20261024T204638Z/step5.log:27`: `PASS no write of the node completed on the late path while its REGISTER was unresolved (writes_done)`
- Batch lap log `tests/evidence/batch_laps/b20261024-6e9fc4/20261024T205839Z-201e9329.log:29`: the same PASS line

The two checks the controls failed do not measure a write:

- `held=0` follows from how arm 0 is built (`tests/late_register_hold_lap.sh:62` `PF_KNOBS="pr_fill_bounded=1 pr_fill_hold=${LRH_ARM:?}"`, `pal/linux/kern.c:7329` `if (fence_ms && READ_ONCE(mxfs_pr_fill_hold)) {`). It is the switch's setting, not something the rig observed.
- `requests_sent` is the change in `iorequest_cnt` over a 15000 ms window (`pal/linux/kern.c:7368-7372`). By the oracle's own wording (`:40`) this includes multipathd's TEST UNIT READY. The checker interval was `step5.log:7` `INFO test16 polling_interval=2 s, outstanding window 15000 ms, pr_fill_hold=0`, and 15 s / 2 s gives 7-8 checks, exactly the 7-8 measured. Nothing in the evidence shows any of those commands to be a write of the node, and `writes_done=0` shows that none of them wrote anything.

So the controls did not reproduce the mechanism the cause names: no write of the node was queued or completed down the late path while the REGISTER was unresolved. The cause comes from reading the code, not from the instrument.

The record's own defect was never instrumented either:

- `tests/late_register_hold_lap.sh:18`: `# A REGISTER held at the target past a fence is not reproduced; the module's`
- `tests/late_register_hold_lap.sh:128-129`: the lap is UNMEASURED unless `register_rc` is 0. The window is therefore taken after a REGISTER that answered GOOD (`pal/linux/kern.c:7364-7371`, the `msleep(ms)` comes after `mxfs_prout_path` returned). It never covers a REGISTER with an unknown outcome. The quarantine branch under the hold (`kern.c:7380-7390`: OFFLINE, then `mxfs_pr_path_release`) never ran, and no lap includes a fence.
- The ruling names this measurement as required: `docs/rulings/silent-peer-death-under-the-stall-limit.md:309-310` `a REGISTER held until after the certificate` / `and then released; a fence during every phase of a retry or handoff;`

## Check 1a: the fix is not the ruling's closure the proposal says it is. FAILS

The argument cites ":300-306 ... its second closure: a returning path carries none of the node's I/O while its late REGISTER is unresolved". The ruling's three closures are:

- `docs/rulings/silent-peer-death-under-the-stall-limit.md:302-305`: `Either registrations are created only while` / `the incarnation is not yet fenceable and reconnects only verify retained ones, or an unresolved` / `REGISTER keeps the fence from certifying until it is resolved, or the fence aborts the tasks of` / `every nexus of the logical unit.`

The second closure there is that the fence does not certify. The fix does not do that. It leaves in place exactly the restoration the ruling warns about (`:301-302` `puts the fenced key back on its nexus, and no later READ KEYS` / `can tell`). Its safety argument rests on an assumption the record itself marks as unmeasured. The record's `next` says "What it rests on: a request the initiator completed before the REGISTER was sent (answered, or ended by error handling) does not execute at the target after it". No run measures that.

## Check 3: the fix-on batch is sized from the control and passed every lap with exposure. Arithmetic holds, standing does not

- Recomputed: the one-sided lower bound for 10 of 10 at alpha 0.005 is 0.005^(1/10) = 0.5887. Then N = ceil(ln 0.005 / ln(1 - 0.5887)) = ceil(5.96) = 6. This matches `step6.log:18` `lower bound 0.5887 at delta 0.005: a verification batch needs 6 clean laps`.
- `tests/evidence/lanes/g2b_a22_20261024T204638Z/step7.log:1` declared before lap 1. `:3-8` six `PASS ... exposure=1`. `:9` `verify b20261024-61fcda: 6 PASS, 0 FAIL of 6 planned`. The same holds for `g2b_a22nm_20261024T213527Z/step6.log:3-8`.
- These passes show only that `requests_sent` dropped from 7-8 to 0 and `held` went to 1. That is, the hold stops the path checker. They cannot show a hazard was removed when the control never showed the hazard.
- The two verify proposals the argument relies on, P20261024-229ee1 and P20261024-cb280d, are `"state": "pending"` (`tools/defects.py proposal show`). There is no review for either in `data/reviews/`, and the record lists both batches as `open`: `batch b20261024-61fcda verify ... 6 laps planned, open, candidate 0.90.134`.

## Check 4: reach of a removal

The removal would dispose of a record that reads `observed 1/any`. Verification ran only at 2 nodes on multipath. The argument limits the precondition to multipath maps by reading code: "The fill runs only for a map registered per path, so the multipath configurations are where its precondition exists". The record's `next` itself says this "needs its own reviewed narrowing before a removal". No such narrowing exists, and nothing was measured at 4 or more nodes.

## Verdict: REJECT

The cause is not proven by instrumented evidence. In all 20 control laps the measured hazard quantity was zero (`writes_done=0`). The checks that failed were the arm's own switch setting (`held=0`) and a command count that matches multipathd's 2 s checker over the 15 s window. The defect on record, a timed-out REGISTER executing after a fence and restoring the key, was never put through a fence, a REGISTER with an unknown outcome, or the quarantine path. The fix is not the ruling's second closure: the ruling's second closure keeps the fence from certifying. The fix's safety rests on an unmeasured assumption about target-side ordering. The verify proposals it relies on are still pending.

What a resubmission needs:

1. A control that shows writes of the node (`writes_done > 0`, or target-side evidence of writes queued behind the REGISTER on that nexus) dispatched down the late path with `pr_fill_hold=0`.
2. A lap that delays a REGISTER at the target until after a peer's PREEMPT AND ABORT and certificate (the ruling at `:309-310`). It must show with the fix off that a node write lands after the fence, and with the fix on that none does.
3. Measured evidence for the ordering assumption the fix rests on.
4. Approved verify reviews.
5. A reviewed reach narrowing, or verification at every configuration the removal disposes of.
