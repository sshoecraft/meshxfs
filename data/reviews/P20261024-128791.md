What it does not prove: the infrastructure fault itself, i.e. **why the pool lock was held for more than 60 s**. A refusal by the lock is the symptom; the cause is whatever held the lock, and no cited file names it. The record the proposal points to says so in its own words:

And an MXFS cause for a long hold is a live alternative, by the tool's own code. Work done while holding the pool lock includes releasing another lane's finished allocation, which unmounts MXFS and unloads the module on that lane's nodes:

- `tools/lun_pool.sh:647` `release "$id" || die "lun$id: its finished allocation of $n was not released"` (inside the lock taken at :604)
- `tools/lun_pool.sh:669` `release "$id" || die "lun$id: the finished allocation was not released"` (same)
- `tools/lun_pool.sh:491` `for n in $nodes; do ( ssh_n "$n" "$snippet" 150 > "$td/$n" ) & done` (release, 150 s per node)
- `tools/lun_pool.sh:465` `NODE_LOGOUT=$NODE_UNMOUNT'`
- `tools/lun_pool.sh:321` `timeout 30 umount "$m" 2>/dev/null && break` (up to 5 tries, `tools/lun_pool.sh:318`)

An MXFS umount on another lane's nodes that stalls past 60 s while the pool lock is held would produce exactly this refusal, and would itself be an MXFS stall above `stall_limit_s` (30 s), i.e. a stability defect, not rig. Nothing cited excludes it.

That fits the record's guess (a lane booting nodes inside the lock), but it does not name the holder or the operation, and it does not rule out a release (MXFS umount/rmmod) inside the lock. The proposal's sentence "Other rig lanes (g2c, g2d, g2e, g4, g4c, g8b) were preparing their own pool LUNs in the same minutes" cites no file, and preparing near the same time does not identify the holder.

## Verdict: REJECT

The run was refused by the host-side pool flock (`prep_1.log:4`). The proposal does not prove why the flock was held for more than 60 s, and the holder is named nowhere. The record it cites says so itself. Work done under that lock includes MXFS umount and rmmod on other lanes' nodes (`tools/lun_pool.sh:647`, `:669`, `:321`), so an MXFS stall is a live cause, and nothing excludes it. The run captured no kernel logs, so "no MXFS signature" is unmeasured.

What would let this be approved:

This could be that lane's run or prep log with its pool-lock timing, or the holder logging proposed in the record's `next`. That evidence must show the hold was not a `release` blocked on an MXFS umount, an rmmod or an unresponsive MXFS node.
2. Kernel logs for the run's window from test1/test2, and from the holder's nodes if the hold involved them, showing none of `BUG:`, `Oops`, `hung_task`, `blocked for more than`, `Shutting down filesystem`, `Corruption` or a fence.
