## What the fix-on batch exercises, and what it does not

Both batches exercise ccph() itself (:199) and the call sites :295 `ccph fill` (both ranks) and :364 `ccph start` (rank 1).

The calls at :392, :407, :417, :428, :513 and :516 come after the `aw_start` rendezvous (:365), which no lap passed. They are unexercised, and the proposal's `change.cause` says so explicitly ("reached by neither batch and no fix of them is claimed").

This does not leave the record's defect open. Any timeout after :295 now finds at least the `fill` marker, and verified at :364 the `start` marker, so a timed-out row can no longer be read as `none` from that point on. If one of the later calls were faulty, the census would name an earlier phase. It could not read `none`, and that would be a different defect.

The window before :295 (alloc_witness.sh:201-294: mount check, witness-file enumeration, rank 1's `mkdir`/`sync -f` at :294) writes no marker, so a row timing out there would still read `none`. That window holds no rendezvous and no wait on a peer. The record's observed failures, and this lap, are timeouts after the fill. This is noted for whoever next touches the script; it is not a condition of this disposition.
