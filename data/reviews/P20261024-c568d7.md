### What the cited evidence contradicts: the power-up the proposal names cannot account for the 58 s

The proposal says the 58 s was lane g4c's power-up:

- `tests/evidence/lanes/g4c_a17tf_20261024T104047Z/summary.log:8` `2026-10-24T10:40:47Z step 1 start: scripts/lab_power.sh up group:g4c`, `:9` `2026-10-24T10:41:37Z step 1 end rc=0 50s: scripts/lab_power.sh up group:g4c`
- `tests/evidence/lanes/g4c_a17tf_20261024T104047Z/step1.log:1` `[10:40:47] host power-up lock held (waited 0 s)`, `:6` `[10:41:37] up 4 domain(s): ok`

g4c's `lab_power.sh` exited, and released its lock, at 10:41:37. But pw8c4's wait started no earlier than 10:40:48 (`prep.log:6`; `t0` is set inside the preflight, after its kernel-health and D-state checks at `prep.log:12-46`), so a 58 s wait ended no earlier than **10:41:46**, at least 9 s after g4c let go. Another measured run confirms the power-up lock was still held exclusive after 10:41:37:

- `tests/evidence/20261024T104144Z_fourkn_merge_2-disk-caw-direct4k/preflight.txt:1` `=== clyde_preflight (2026-10-24T10:41:44Z) ===`
- `tests/evidence/20261024T104144Z_fourkn_merge_2-disk-caw-direct4k/preflight.txt:39` `  waited 2 s for a power-up to finish`

A shared `flock` blocks only on an exclusive holder, so some process held `/tmp/mxfs_host_powerup.lock` exclusive at or after 10:41:44. It was not g4c's power-up, and the proposal does not name it. The host journal (not cited by the proposer) shows a domain being started at that moment: `journalctl -k --utc` `Oct 24 10:41:37 clyde kernel: br0: port 22(vnet90) entered blocking state`, followed by vnet91-94 on br0/mxsana/mxsanb. That points to a second `lab_power.sh up` that took the lock as g4c released it, but which caller, which domains, and whether every domain it waited on was booting (rather than a running node whose `wait_ssh`, `scripts/lab_power.sh:84`, was stalled) is nowhere in the evidence.

This gap is not incidental. It is the part of the hold that pushed it past the run's 60 s bound. With the power-up lock free at 10:41:37, pw8c4's preflight would have finished its 3 s log sample and remaining checks at about 10:41:42 (compare `tests/evidence/20261024T104137Z_tfshell_tff2/prep.log:7` and `:90`: a whole preflight with no wait took 10:41:56 to 10:42:04). This run would then have had the pool lock at about 48 s of its 60. The unnamed holder of the power-up lock from 10:41:37 to at least 10:41:46 is the proximate cause of the refusal. The previous review (`data/reviews/P20261024-128791.md:78`) asked for the holder and its operation, and for evidence that the hold "was not a `release` blocked on an MXFS umount, an rmmod or an unresponsive MXFS node". The proposal has moved that question down one lock rather than answered it. The proposal's sentence "The hold was a preflight ... waiting on a VM boot" is true for 10:40:48 to 10:41:37 by g4c's logs. For the decisive remainder it is inferred, from the code saying only `lab_power.sh up` takes the lock exclusive, not measured.

Uncited statement: "In the host kernel log for 10:40:40-10:42:00Z, the only SCSI target session events are ..." cites no file. As far as I read the host journal (10:41:30-10:41:56Z), I found test7/test8 logins at 10:41:34-35 (`Oct 24 10:41:34 clyde kernel: ... scst_init_session:7164:Using security group "alloc" for initiator "iqn.2004-10.com.ubuntu:01:test8-mxfs-node"`) and the domain start at 10:41:37 quoted above.

**Result: not proven.** The pool-lock holder (pw8c4's host preflight under `lun_pool.sh quiet`) and the exclusion of any release under that lock are proven by measurement. The holder of the host power-up lock that made the hold exceed 60 s is not named, and the proposal attributes it to a power-up that, by its own cited logs, had already ended.

## Verdict: REJECT

The proposal now proves who held the pool lock (pw8c4's host preflight, `tests/evidence/20261024T104044Z_peerwin_pw8c4/prep.log:6`, `:62`) and that no release ran under it. test1/test2's kernel logs for the window show no MXFS signature. But the preflight's 58 s wait (`prep.log:51`) cannot have been g4c's power-up alone. That power-up ended at 10:41:37 (`tests/evidence/lanes/g4c_a17tf_20261024T104047Z/summary.log:9`), while the wait ended no earlier than 10:41:46. `tests/evidence/20261024T104144Z_fourkn_merge_2-disk-caw-direct4k/preflight.txt:39` shows an exclusive holder still there after 10:41:44. That unnamed holder accounts for exactly the seconds that took the pool-lock hold past this run's 60 s bound, and the proposal attributes them to a power-up its own cited logs show had already finished.
