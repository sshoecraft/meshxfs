  depends entirely on the branch that was never measured.

## Verdict: REJECT

- **Check 1: MET.** The cause is measured. The rules omit included headers, and with the fix out, 4 shared objects
  stay stale after a header change, 10 of 10.
- **Check 2: MET.** The control ran 10 of 10 exposed FAILs with exactly the fix block switched off, on inputs whose
  hashes equal the freeze.
- **Check 3: MET for the `.d`-present branch.** N = 6 is correct. 6 of 6 exposed PASSes on 0.90.134, none failed and
  none was dropped.
- **Check 4: NOT MET.**
  - The removal claims `tests/tauth/Makefile:188-189` rebuild an object that has no `.d`.
  - That is the only mechanism protecting the 18 objects the pre-fix Makefile left in the candidate's tree.
  - Five of those objects are older than the last write of `pal/pal.h`, and none has a `.d`.
  - No lap exercised that branch.

What would satisfy this:
1. A lap, or a variant of this lap, that leaves objects without `.d` files before the headers are marked. For example,
   the first build with `TAUTH_DEPS=0`, or the objects as the pre-fix rules build them. Then it marks `pal/pal.h` and
   `dlm/dlm.h`, runs a plain `make all` with the fix on, and requires the marks as `:93-102` do now.
2. A control of that lap with the fix off for the second build too. `b20261023-af4605` already measures that arm, if
   the lap is otherwise the same. Then a verification sized from it, on the candidate.
3. Alternatively, evidence already in hand that shows a pre-fix object with no `.d` rebuilt by a plain make after a
   header change, cited by `file:line`.
