Instance B, `tests/evidence/reap_inactive_mount_ab/rfkb/control_console_tail.txt`:
- `:502` `CPU: 1 PID: 3943 Comm: lvm Not tainted 6.8.0-101-generic #101-Ubuntu`
- `:507-508` `RAX: 000000000001ffff RBX: 0000000000000000` ... `RDX: 0000000000020000 RSI: 0000000000000000`
- `:519-522` `bio_endio+0x119/0x1d0`, `submit_bio_noacct+0x1a7/0x5c0`, `? __req_mod+0x843/0xa60 [drbd]`, `drbd_send_and_submit+0x25b/0x830 [drbd]`

In both field instances the backing bio was failed by `submit_bio_noacct` itself. It completed inside its own submission, in the issuing `lvm` task, so the backing device was already refusing bios when DRBD sent the read. The registers that hold sector and size are the same in both (RBX/RSI `0`, RDX/R13 `0x20000`), which fits a 128 KiB read at sector 0, lvm's label read. That inline failure is what the record's summary names: "an LVM scan's direct read of the DRBD device failed inside the backing device's submission".

In all six tries the backing bio was accepted and queued. It then failed later, at dispatch, in a `kblockd` worker, after `device offline error`, while the session was being logged out. Each failing read was one of fio's 4 KiB reads (RDX/R13 `0x1000`, random sector). In none of the six was the failing read the `lvm pvscan --cache` loop's read, and in none was it failed inside its own submission. The script's own premise is contradicted by what it measured: `tests/drbd_backing_cut_repro.sh:13-14` `A block device whose iSCSI session is gone fails every bio inside its submission, which is the precondition both oopses share.`

The proposal says the tries show "the same completion chain (__req_mod+0x269 <- drbd_request_endio <- bio_endio)". That holds for the three frames it quotes and for nothing below them. The proposal does not mention the different entry, task, size or failure point.

What the six tries prove is that the stock drbd module dereferences NULL at `__drbd_change_sync+0x56` when a backing read completes with an error, with no MeshXFS anywhere on the pair. They do not reproduce the record's precondition, a backing device that was already dead at submission while DRBD still held it attached, with MeshXFS absent.

## Check 4: does the evidence exclude an MXFS sequence as the cause of instance A's failed backing bio?

No. Instance A's boot had the module loaded, and it had been reloaded once in that boot: `netconsole.log:96208` `Modules linked in: ... mxfs(OE) ... [last unloaded: mxfs(OE)]`. The tries show that DRBD crashes once a backing read fails. They do not show what failed A's backing read, and none of the cited evidence speaks to that.

The record's own next step sets this bar. It says the outcome is "either a DRBD driver fault reached by the rig with MXFS absent (remove --disproved on that direct evidence) or a sequence an MXFS install can produce, which keeps it open". The proposal answers the second half by assertion: "what the rig did that cut a backing session under DRBD is the rig's matter, not this record's". No cited file shows that the rig, and not MeshXFS, cut instance A's backing session.

An MXFS sequence that does this kind of cut is in the tree, and it is one of the two files the a22d lane had to park:
- `tools/mxfs_fenced_path_reset.sh:57` `iscsiadm -m session -r "$sid" --logout > /dev/null 2>&1 &&` and `:58` `iscsiadm -m node -T "$tgt" -p "$addr:$port" --login > /dev/null 2>&1; then`. This is an iSCSI logout, the same cut the tries used (`tests/drbd_backing_cut_repro.sh:177`). A logged-out session's disk is the "gone at submission" state of instances A and B.
- `pal/linux/kern.c:5974` `rc = call_usermodehelper(argv[0], argv, envp, UMH_WAIT_EXEC);` runs it from mxfs.ko when the node is fenced.
- `tools/mxfs_drbd_fence_self.py:833` `subprocess.Popen(["/bin/sh", "-c", "sleep 1; drbdadm disconnect %s; %s"` changes DRBD state from MXFS's DRBD fence path. The review of P20261024-c64f70 already named this path (`data/reviews/P20261024-c64f70.md:58-59`).

Whether the path-reset helper can name a DRBD backing disk's path (`pal/linux/kern.c:5948-5965`) is a question for a measurement, not for code reading. That measurement could be a `P-FENCED-PATH-RESET dev=drbd...` line from a fenced DRBD-rig node (`kern.c:5962` logs `no SCSI path -- nothing to reset`; `:5975` logs the paths it reset), or a fence on a DRBD pair. No such line is cited.

## Verdict: REJECT

What is proven, and stands for the next proposal:
- On test29/test30, with no mxfs module, rule, package, unit or binary in either boot (Check 1), logging N1 out of its pool target under O_DIRECT reads of a plain DRBD resource panicked N1 at `__drbd_change_sync+0x56`, 6 times in 6 tries.
- The fault site matches the record's: same RIP, Code bytes, `RDI 0`, `CR2 0x10`, and `__req_mod+0x269` <- `drbd_request_endio+0x99`. The drbd module crashes on a failed backing read without MeshXFS.

What is missing before the record can be removed as disproved:

1. **The record's own path, reproduced with MeshXFS absent.** In both field instances an `lvm` task's direct aio read (128 KiB at sector 0) of the DRBD device has its backing bio failed inside `submit_bio_noacct` (`netconsole.log:96149,96154-96169`; `control_console_tail.txt:502,507-522`). In all six tries a `kblockd` worker failed a queued 4 KiB fio read at dispatch after `device offline error` (each try's `console_test29.txt:2,8,10,14-15,28-36`). Reproduce the inline-submission failure: a backing device already gone (session logged out and its disk removed) while DRBD still holds it, followed by an O_DIRECT read of `/dev/drbdN` (the `lvm pvscan --cache` read). Or show, with measured evidence, that the two entries are one precondition.
2. **What failed instance A's backing bio, or a measured exclusion of MeshXFS's sequences.** Instance A ran with mxfs.ko loaded (`netconsole.log:96208`). Both mxfs.ko's fenced-path reset, which runs an iSCSI logout and login (`tools/mxfs_fenced_path_reset.sh:57-58`, invoked at `pal/linux/kern.c:5974`), and `mxfs_drbd_fence_self.py` (`:833`) are MXFS sequences that can put a DRBD node's backing path or state into the precondition. The record's own next step says such a sequence "keeps it open". Calling the cut "the rig's matter" is an inference. Either identify the cut in A from the rig's or the node's logs, or measure that no MXFS sequence on a DRBD configuration logs out or kills a backing disk's session (for example `P-FENCED-PATH-RESET` lines for a drbd device on a fenced DRBD-rig node).
3. **The rig's side, recorded.** If the rig's pool rebind is what cuts a backing session under a live DRBD (`scripts/drbd_rig.sh:120-122`, quoted in `data/reviews/P20261024-c64f70.md:60`), that is a harness defect. It needs its own record before this one can be called the rig's matter. The proposal names no such record.

The tries prove that DRBD is fragile when its backing disk is lost. They do not prove that the node crash this record reports, on a node with mxfs.ko loaded, was not reached through MeshXFS. Under the release bar, a crash that an MXFS sequence can produce on MXFS's DRBD configuration stays open even when the faulting frame is in DRBD.
